What EMDR feels like

A lot of people have heard of EMDR but aren't sure what actually happens. Here is a plain walk-through of what EMDR can feel like with me.

What EMDR is

EMDR stands for Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing. It helps people process memories that haven't been fully worked through. It uses bilateral stimulation, like eye movements or gentle tapping, while we work with a memory and the belief you formed about yourself because of it.

We don't start with the hard stuff

The first sessions are about getting to know you and getting ready. We talk about your history and what you want help with. Before we get into the hard stuff, we build coping skills that work for you. We practice ways to calm your body and come back to the present when things feel big. You stay in charge of what we work on and when.

What a processing session can feel like

When we do start processing, you bring up part of a memory while following the eye movements or tapping. Then we pause and I ask what you noticed. People notice all kinds of things: a feeling, a picture, a body sensation, a thought, or nothing much at all. There's no right answer. You don't have to tell me every detail of the memory for EMDR to work.

Some people say it feels like watching a memory from a little farther away. Some feel tired afterward. Feelings can come up between sessions too. We plan for that, and we end each session with time to settle.

It's okay to stop

You can pause or stop at any time. Going at a pace that feels safe to you matters more than going fast.

The details

  • EMDR sessions are in person only, at my Murfreesboro office on Tuesdays.
