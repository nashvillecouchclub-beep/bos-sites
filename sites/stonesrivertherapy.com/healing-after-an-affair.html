Healing after an affair: where couples start

Finding out about an affair can turn your world upside down. You may feel shock, anger, grief, numbness, or all of it in one hour. If you're the partner who had the affair, you may feel guilt, shame, or panic about what happens next. Both of you are hurting, in different ways.

You don't have to decide everything right now

Many couples feel pressure to know right away if they'll stay together. You don't have to answer that in the first week, or the first month. It's okay to slow down.

What tends to help early on

  • Getting safe and steady. Sleep, food and support from a trusted friend matter more than people think.
  • Ending contact with the affair partner, if you're trying to rebuild.
  • Not having every big talk at midnight. Pick times when you're both calm enough to listen.
  • Getting support. A couples therapist can help you have the hard talks without them turning into the same fight over and over.

How I work with affairs

I've completed Level 1 training in Gottman Method Couples Therapy and the Gottman Institute's Treating Affairs and Trauma training. That training lays out a path that many couples follow: first making sense of what happened and the hurt it caused, then slowly rebuilding trust, then building a relationship that feels new.

It's not a fast process, and it doesn't look the same for everyone. Some couples rebuild. Some decide to part. Either way, the goal is that you both come out of this with more clarity and less chaos.

I'm also EMDR-trained. Sometimes an affair stirs up old hurts, and individual work can help with that alongside couples sessions.

What the start looks like

Couples work with me begins with 2 to 3 assessment sessions. We meet together and each of you meets with me alone. Then we build a plan with goals in your own words.
